Structural drying is the most important phase of water damage restoration, and the one untrained responders most often get wrong. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

The structural drying technicians at Madera Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Our structural drying process includes daily moisture monitoring with calibrated moisture meters, thermo-hygrometers and thermal imaging cameras. The result is a detailed log of readings that tracks how moisture comes down over time in each material that got wet. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Structural Drying, Step by Step

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's what you can expect when you choose Madera Restoration:

1

Moisture Assessment

Using moisture meters, thermal imaging and hygrometers, we create a comprehensive moisture map of all affected areas. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline readings.

2

Equipment Deployment

Our crew sets up high-velocity air movers along with commercial LGR units in the positions the moisture map calls for. Temperature and airflow are optimized for the specific materials being dried.

3

Daily Checks and Adjustments

Each day a technician visits to document progress, reposition equipment and measure moisture at every monitoring point. You receive updates on the drying timeline.

4

Verifying the Dry Standard

When moisture readings reach acceptable levels throughout all affected materials, we document final readings, remove equipment and certify that your property has reached dry standard.

Maintenance Tips for Structural Drying

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Dehumidifiers lose efficiency when outside air gets in, so keep drying areas closed up
  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Leave equipment where the technician placed it, since positions are based on the moisture map
  • Make sure the technician can get in each day for monitoring visits
  • New odors or wet areas during the drying period should be reported to your technician right away

How to Tell You Need Structural Drying

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Beads of moisture on windows and cold surfaces after the water event
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or blisters after a leak
  • Musty odors that continue after extraction, which suggests trapped moisture
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration

The Benefits of Professional Structural Drying

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.
